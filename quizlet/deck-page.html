1Anatomical Terminology of the Trunk

  1. TrunkThe torso: the central axis of the body
  2. Boundaries of the trunkInferior to the neck, superior to the lower limbs, and between the upper limbs
  3. The 5 major subdivisions of the trunkThorax, Abdomen, Pelvis, Perineum, Back (dorsal trunk)
  4. ThoraxThe chest cavity: ribs, sternum, heart, lungs
  5. AbdomenThe region between thorax and pelvis: the digestive tract
  6. PelvisInferior region within the pelvic girdle: bladder, rectum, reproductive organs
  7. PerineumDiamond-shaped region on the inferior pelvic floor: external genitalia and anus
  8. Back (dorsal trunk)Posterior region housing the vertebral column and spinal muscles
  9. Anterior surface landmarks of the trunkSternal, pectoral, mammary, umbilical, inguinal
  10. Posterior surface landmarks of the trunkVertebral, scapular, lumbar, sacral, gluteal
  11. SternalBreastbone (anterior)
  12. PectoralChest (anterior)
  13. MammaryBreast (anterior)
  14. UmbilicalNavel (anterior)
  15. InguinalGroin (anterior)
  16. VertebralSpine (posterior)
  17. ScapularShoulder blade (posterior)
  18. LumbarLower back (posterior)
  19. SacralBetween the hips (posterior)
  20. GlutealButtock (posterior)
  21. Superior vs. InferiorSuperior = towards the head; Inferior = towards the feet
  22. Anterior (ventral) vs. Posterior (dorsal)Anterior = front; Posterior = back
  23. Medial vs. LateralMedial = closer to the midline; Lateral = further from the midline
  24. Ipsilateral vs. ContralateralIpsilateral = same side of the body; Contralateral = opposite sides
  25. Superficial vs. DeepSuperficial = closer to the skin surface; Deep = further inward
  26. Parietal vs. VisceralParietal = lining the wall of a body cavity; Visceral = directly covering an internal organ
  27. Median (midsagittal) planeVertical plane dividing the trunk into equal right and left halves
  28. Sagittal planeVertical plane parallel to the median, dividing into unequal right and left parts
  29. Coronal (frontal) planeVertical plane dividing into anterior and posterior parts
  30. Transverse (axial) planeHorizontal plane dividing into superior and inferior parts
  31. What does the diaphragm separate?The thoracic cavity from the abdominopelvic cavity
  32. What does the thoracic cavity contain?The pleural and pericardial cavities

2First Week of Human Development

  1. Day 1 of developmentFertilization, in the ampullary region of the uterine tube
  2. Where does fertilization occur?The ampulla of the uterine tube: the widest part, near the ovary
  3. Days 1 to 3 of developmentCleavage: mitotic divisions converting the single zygote into smaller cells
  4. BlastomeresThe progressively smaller cells produced by cleavage of the zygote
  5. Day 3 of developmentMorula: a solid ball of 16 compacted cells, which enters the uterus
  6. MorulaA solid ball of 16 compacted cells, formed on Day 3
  7. Days 4 to 5 of developmentBlastocyst: fluid enters to form a central cavity
  8. Embryoblast (inner cell mass)Located at one pole of the blastocyst; forms the embryo proper
  9. Trophoblast (outer cell mass)The outer epithelial wall of the blastocyst; forms the placenta
  10. Day 6 of developmentImplantation: the blastocyst attaches and embeds into the endometrium
  11. Where does implantation occur?The endometrium of the anterior or posterior uterine wall
  12. Order of first-week eventsFertilization (D1), cleavage (D1 to 3), morula (D3), blastocyst (D4 to 5), implantation (D6)
  13. CapacitationA 7-hour conditioning period in the female reproductive tract that removes the glycoprotein coat and seminal plasma proteins from the acrosome
  14. How long does capacitation take?7 hours
  15. Acrosomal reactionAfter the sperm binds the zona pellucida, enzymes are released to penetrate the egg's coat
  16. Cortical reactionTriggered by sperm entry; prevents polyspermy
  17. What prevents polyspermy?The cortical reaction, triggered by sperm entry
  18. Three events triggered by sperm entryThe cortical reaction (prevents polyspermy), completion of meiosis II, and restoration of the diploid chromosome count
  19. Diploid chromosome number restored at fertilization46

3Second to Eighth Week (Embryonic Period)

  1. The two layers of the trophoblastCytotrophoblast (inner) and syncytiotrophoblast (outer)
  2. CytotrophoblastInner layer of mononucleated cells
  3. SyncytiotrophoblastOuter multinucleated zone without distinct cell boundaries; erodes maternal tissue and sinusoids, and secretes hCG
  4. Which layer secretes hCG?The syncytiotrophoblast
  5. Which layer establishes primitive uteroplacental circulation?The syncytiotrophoblast, by eroding maternal tissue and sinusoids
  6. Bilaminar discEpiblast plus hypoblast, formed from the embryoblast in week 2
  7. EpiblastHigh columnar cells adjacent to the amniotic cavity; gives rise to ALL three primary germ layers
  8. HypoblastSmall cuboidal cells adjacent to the blastocyst cavity and yolk sac
  9. Which layer gives rise to all 3 germ layers?The epiblast
  10. GastrulationWeek 3: establishes all three germ layers by invagination through the primitive streak on the epiblast
  11. The three germ layersEctoderm, mesoderm, endoderm
  12. EXAM TRAP: failure of the primitive streak to degenerateSacrococcygeal teratoma
  13. Sacrococcygeal teratoma is caused byFailure of the primitive streak to degenerate
  14. NotochordMidline cellular rod that provides structural support and induces the overlying ectoderm to undergo neurulation
  15. Neurulation sequenceNeural plate, then neural groove, then neural tube
  16. What does the neural tube form?The central nervous system: brain and spinal cord
  17. Neural crest cellsDetach during neural fold closure and migrate to form the PNS (sensory and autonomic ganglia), melanocytes, Schwann cells and the adrenal medulla
  18. Ectoderm derivativesNervous system (CNS and PNS), epidermis of skin, hair, nails, sweat glands, adrenal medulla
  19. Mesoderm derivativesBones, cartilage, connective tissue, muscle (skeletal, cardiac, smooth), cardiovascular system, kidneys, gonads
  20. Endoderm derivativesEpithelial lining of the GI tract, respiratory tract (trachea, lungs), liver, pancreas, urinary bladder
  21. Paraxial mesodermSegments into somites: about 3 pairs per day, 42 to 44 pairs in total
  22. How many somite pairs form?42 to 44 pairs, at about 3 pairs per day
  23. The three parts of a somiteSclerotome, myotome, dermatome
  24. SclerotomeBones and cartilage of the vertebral column
  25. MyotomeSkeletal muscle
  26. DermatomeDermis of the skin
  27. Intermediate mesodermForms the urogenital system: kidneys and gonads
  28. Lateral plate mesodermSplits into a splanchnic (visceral) layer and a somatic (parietal) layer

4Development of the Musculoskeletal System

  1. Skeletal muscle originParaxial mesoderm (somite myotomes) and the pharyngeal arches
  2. Cardiac muscle originSplanchnic mesoderm
  3. Smooth muscle originSplanchnic mesoderm (viscera) and local mesenchyme (blood vessels)
  4. HIGH-YIELD EXCEPTION: which muscles are NOT derived from mesoderm?Smooth muscle of the iris (sphincter and dilator pupillae) and the sweat and mammary glands: these come from ECTODERM
  5. Origin of the iris sphincter and dilator pupillaeEctoderm, not mesoderm
  6. When do the epimere and hypomere divide?Week 5
  7. Epimere (dorsal division)Forms the deep extensor muscles of the back, such as erector spinae; innervated by dorsal primary rami
  8. Hypomere (ventral division)Forms anterior and lateral body wall muscles (intercostals, obliques, rectus abdominis) and the limb muscles; innervated by ventral primary rami
  9. Which muscles do the dorsal primary rami supply?Epimere derivatives: the deep extensor muscles of the back
  10. Which muscles do the ventral primary rami supply?Hypomere derivatives: body wall and limb muscles
  11. Tongue muscles are derived fromOccipital myotomes
  12. Extraocular eye muscles are derived fromPreoptic myotomes
  13. Muscles of mastication, facial expression, pharynx and larynx are derived fromPharyngeal arch mesenchyme

5Human Birth Defects (Teratology)

  1. TeratogenAn environmental agent that causes congenital anomalies
  2. Weeks 1 to 2 (pre-embryonic period)The all-or-none period: death or total repair
  3. Weeks 3 to 8 (embryonic period)PEAK SENSITIVITY: organogenesis occurs here, so exposure leads to major structural malformations
  4. Weeks 9 to 38 (fetal period)Exposure causes functional deficits or minor growth changes
  5. When is the peak teratogen period?Weeks 3 to 8, the embryonic period
  6. AlcoholFetal alcohol syndrome: the number one preventable cause of intellectual disability
  7. Fetal alcohol syndrome featuresMicrocephaly, flat philtrum, thin upper lip
  8. Number one preventable cause of intellectual disabilityFetal alcohol syndrome
  9. ThalidomideSevere limb defects: amelia, or phocomelia and meromelia
  10. AmeliaMissing limbs
  11. Phocomelia / meromeliaSeal-like, shortened limbs
  12. Rubella virusClassic triad of bilateral cataracts, sensorineural deafness and cardiac defects
  13. Nicotine and smokingLow birth weight and orofacial clefts (cleft lip and palate)
  14. What causes numerical chromosomal anomalies?Meiotic nondisjunction
  15. Down syndromeTrisomy 21
  16. Turner syndrome45,X: the only viable monosomy in humans. Females with short stature, webbed neck and broad chest
  17. Only viable monosomy in humansTurner syndrome (45,X)
  18. Klinefelter syndrome47,XXY: male with tall stature, gynecomastia and testicular atrophy
  19. Cri-du-chat syndromeTerminal deletion of chromosome 5p: cat-like cry and microcephaly
  20. AchondroplasiaAutosomal dominant mutation in FGFR3 causing short-limb dwarfism
  21. Which gene is mutated in achondroplasia?FGFR3
  22. Structural chromosomal anomaly exampleCri-du-chat syndrome: a terminal deletion of chromosome 5p
