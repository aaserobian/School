Teratogen	An environmental agent that causes congenital anomalies
Weeks 1 to 2 (pre-embryonic period)	The all-or-none period: death or total repair
Weeks 3 to 8 (embryonic period)	PEAK SENSITIVITY: organogenesis occurs here, so exposure leads to major structural malformations
Weeks 9 to 38 (fetal period)	Exposure causes functional deficits or minor growth changes
When is the peak teratogen period?	Weeks 3 to 8, the embryonic period
Alcohol	Fetal alcohol syndrome: the number one preventable cause of intellectual disability
Fetal alcohol syndrome features	Microcephaly, flat philtrum, thin upper lip
Number one preventable cause of intellectual disability	Fetal alcohol syndrome
Thalidomide	Severe limb defects: amelia, or phocomelia and meromelia
Amelia	Missing limbs
Phocomelia / meromelia	Seal-like, shortened limbs
Rubella virus	Classic triad of bilateral cataracts, sensorineural deafness and cardiac defects
Nicotine and smoking	Low birth weight and orofacial clefts (cleft lip and palate)
What causes numerical chromosomal anomalies?	Meiotic nondisjunction
Down syndrome	Trisomy 21
Turner syndrome	45,X: the only viable monosomy in humans. Females with short stature, webbed neck and broad chest
Only viable monosomy in humans	Turner syndrome (45,X)
Klinefelter syndrome	47,XXY: male with tall stature, gynecomastia and testicular atrophy
Cri-du-chat syndrome	Terminal deletion of chromosome 5p: cat-like cry and microcephaly
Achondroplasia	Autosomal dominant mutation in FGFR3 causing short-limb dwarfism
Which gene is mutated in achondroplasia?	FGFR3
Structural chromosomal anomaly example	Cri-du-chat syndrome: a terminal deletion of chromosome 5p
