Tight (occluding) junction	The apical seal that prevents paracellular leakage and maintains polarity
Tight junction link proteins	Occludins, claudins and ZO proteins
Tight junction cytoskeletal anchor	Actin
Which pathogen targets tight junctions?	Clostridium perfringens enterotoxin, giving a leaky epithelium and watery diarrhea
Adherens junction	A continuous belt below the tight junctions that stabilizes sheets and initiates cell contact
Adherens junction link proteins	Cadherins, which are calcium-dependent
Adherens junction cytoskeletal anchor	Actin
Desmosome	A spot-weld rivet that resists mechanical shear
Desmosome link proteins	Cadherins
Desmosome cytoskeletal anchor	Keratin intermediate filaments
Which disease targets desmosomes?	Pemphigus vulgaris, giving an intraepidermal blister
Gap junction	Direct cell-to-cell ionic and metabolic communication
Gap junction link proteins	Connexons, built from connexins
Which junction has no cytoskeletal anchor?	The gap junction, because it is pore-forming
Hemidesmosome	Anchors the basal cell surface to the basement membrane
Hemidesmosome link proteins	Integrins
Hemidesmosome cytoskeletal anchor	Keratin intermediate filaments
Which disease targets hemidesmosomes?	Bullous pemphigoid, giving a subepidermal, deeper blister
PemphiGUS vs. Pemphigoid	PemphiGUS hits desmosomes, an intraepidermal blister. PemphigoiD hits hemiDesmosomes, a Deeper subepidermal blister.
Which junctions anchor to actin?	Tight junctions and adherens junctions
Which junctions anchor to keratin?	Desmosomes and hemidesmosomes
Cardiac intercalated discs	Gap junctions for the electrical syncytium, fasciae adherens to transmit contractile force, and desmosomes for mechanical anchoring
Which junction makes the heart an electrical syncytium?	The gap junction
Simple epithelium	One layer, for absorption and secretion
Stratified epithelium	Multiple layers, for protection
Pseudostratified epithelium	A single layer that looks stratified; every cell touches the basement membrane
Transitional epithelium	Stretches to accommodate volume
The three epithelial cell shapes	Squamous (flat), cuboidal (cube), columnar (tall)
Squamous	Flat
Cuboidal	Cube-shaped
Columnar	Tall
Which epithelium lines the urinary bladder?	Transitional
Which epithelium lines the airway?	Pseudostratified ciliated, with goblet cells
Merocrine secretion	Secretion by exocytosis, with the cell left completely intact, as in eccrine sweat glands
Apocrine secretion	The apical portion of cytoplasm is pinched off with the product, as in milk fat secretion
Holocrine secretion	The entire cell ruptures and disintegrates to release its product, as in sebaceous glands
Mnemonic for the secretion modes	MEROcrine is Mere exocytosis. APOcrine is APex pinched off. HOLOcrine is HOLe cell lost.
Which glands secrete by holocrine?	Sebaceous glands
Which glands secrete by merocrine?	Eccrine sweat glands
