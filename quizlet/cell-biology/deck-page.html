1.1Cell theory foundations

3 clinical
  1. The cellThe basic structural and functional unit of life
  2. Cell theoryAll organisms consist of one or more cells, and all cells arise from pre-existing cells
  3. Where does function come from?Molecular and organelle structure
  4. At what level is homeostasis maintained?The cellular level
  5. Clinical principle of cell theoryclinicalDisease is cellular dysfunction made visible
  6. A missing enzyme leads toclinicalA storage disease
  7. A failed junction leads toclinicalA leaky epithelium

1.2The three cell compartments

  1. The three major cell compartmentsPlasma membrane, cytoplasm, nucleus
  2. Plasma membraneA selectively permeable fluid-mosaic lipid bilayer with embedded proteins
  3. What does the plasma membrane control?Transport, cell signaling and cell adhesion
  4. CytoplasmCytosol, organelles, cytoskeleton and inclusions
  5. InclusionsStored cytoplasmic material such as glycogen or lipid droplets
  6. NucleusEnclosed by a nuclear envelope; holds the genome and nucleolus, and is the site of DNA and RNA synthesis
  7. The human genome22 autosomes plus XX or XY

1.3Organelle functions

  1. CytosolSite of metabolic pathways and of protein synthesis on free ribosomes
  2. Free ribosomesTranslate mRNA into cytosolic proteins
  3. Rough endoplasmic reticulum (RER)Studded with ribosomes; translates mRNA into membrane-bound and secreted proteins
  4. Smooth endoplasmic reticulum (SER)Synthesizes lipids and steroids, detoxifies substances, and stores calcium
  5. Golgi apparatusModifies, sorts and packages proteins and lipids for delivery
  6. Mannose-6-phosphatemnemonicThe Golgi tag that routes acid hydrolases to the lysosomes
  7. MitochondriaGenerate ATP by oxidative phosphorylation and store calcium
  8. Mitochondrial DNACircular, and inherited from the mother only
  9. LysosomesMembrane-bound sacs of acid hydrolases for intracellular degradation
  10. Internal pH of a lysosomeAbout 5
  11. AutophagyLysosomal degradation of worn-out organelles
  12. What reaches lysosomes by endocytosis or phagocytosis?Engulfed extracellular material
  13. EndosomesUptake and sorting of material internalized from the cell surface
  14. PeroxisomesDetoxification and beta-oxidation of very-long-chain fatty acids
  15. ProteasomeDegrades intracellular proteins tagged with ubiquitin, for proteome quality control
  16. UbiquitinmnemonicThe tag that marks cytosolic proteins for destruction by the proteasome
  17. Which two organelles store calcium?The smooth ER and the mitochondria

  2. What do microfilaments do?Cell shape, contraction, motility, and anchoring junctions
  3. Intermediate filamentsAbout 10 nm across; provide mechanical strength
  4. Intermediate filament tissue markersKeratin in epithelia, desmin in muscle, GFAP in glia, neurofilaments in neurons
  5. KeratinThe intermediate filament of epithelia
  6. DesminThe intermediate filament of muscle
  7. GFAPThe intermediate filament of glia
  8. NeurofilamentsThe intermediate filaments of neurons
  9. MicrotubulesAbout 25 nm across, made of tubulin
  10. What do microtubules do?Tracks for organelle transport, the cores of cilia and flagella, and the mitotic spindle
  11. Which drug targets microtubules?clinicalColchicine
  12. Diameters of the three cytoskeletal elementsMicrofilaments about 7 nm, intermediate filaments about 10 nm, microtubules about 25 nm

2.1The endomembrane pathway

  1. The secretory pathway, in orderDNA transcribed to mRNA in the nucleus, translated on RER ribosomes, carried by vesicle to the Golgi for modification and packaging, then by vesicle to the plasma membrane or the lysosomes
  2. Step 1 of the secretory pathwayDNA is transcribed to mRNA in the nucleus
  3. Where are secreted proteins translated?On the ribosomes of the rough ER
  4. What does the Golgi contribute to the secretory pathway?Modification, sorting and packaging, then dispatch by vesicle
  5. The two destinations of Golgi vesiclesThe plasma membrane, for secretion by exocytosis, or the lysosomes
  6. RER-rich cellsPlasma cells and pancreatic acinar cells, built for heavy protein secretion
  7. SER-rich cellsAdrenal cortex, gonads and liver cells, built for lipid and steroid synthesis and detoxification
  8. Which cells are packed with SER, and why?Adrenal cortex, gonads and liver, because they make steroids and lipids and detoxify

2.2Membrane proteins and transport

  1. The six functional roles of membrane proteinsTransport, enzymatic activity, signal transduction, cell-cell recognition, intercellular joining, and attachment or anchoring
  2. Mnemonic for membrane protein functionsmnemonicGET a CHILLED
  3. ChannelsWater-filled passive pores, such as aquaporins and ion channels
  4. Carriers (transporters)Bind solutes and undergo conformational changes; passive or active, such as the GLUT transporters
  5. Pumps (ATPases)Consume ATP directly to move solutes against a concentration gradient
  6. Channel vs. carrier vs. pumpChannel: a passive pore. Carrier: binds and changes shape. Pump: spends ATP to go against the gradient.
  7. AquaporinsChannel proteins for water
  8. GLUT transportersCarrier proteins for glucose

  2. Tight junction link proteinsOccludins, claudins and ZO proteins
  3. Tight junction cytoskeletal anchorActin
  4. Which pathogen targets tight junctions?clinicalClostridium perfringens enterotoxin, giving a leaky epithelium and watery diarrhea

  14. Which junction has no cytoskeletal anchor?The gap junction, because it is pore-forming
  15. HemidesmosomeAnchors the basal cell surface to the basement membrane

  20. Which junctions anchor to actin?Tight junctions and adherens junctions

3.2Epithelial classification

  1. Simple epitheliumOne layer, for absorption and secretion
  2. Stratified epitheliumMultiple layers, for protection
  3. Pseudostratified epitheliumA single layer that looks stratified; every cell touches the basement membrane
  4. Transitional epitheliumStretches to accommodate volume
  5. The three epithelial cell shapesSquamous (flat), cuboidal (cube), columnar (tall)
  6. SquamousFlat
  7. CuboidalCube-shaped
  8. ColumnarTall
  9. Which epithelium lines the urinary bladder?Transitional
  10. Which epithelium lines the airway?Pseudostratified ciliated, with goblet cells

3.3Modes of exocrine secretion

  1. Merocrine secretionSecretion by exocytosis, with the cell left completely intact, as in eccrine sweat glands
  2. Apocrine secretionThe apical portion of cytoplasm is pinched off with the product, as in milk fat secretion
  3. Holocrine secretionThe entire cell ruptures and disintegrates to release its product, as in sebaceous glands
  4. Mnemonic for the secretion modesmnemonicMEROcrine is Mere exocytosis. APOcrine is APex pinched off. HOLOcrine is HOLe cell lost.
  5. Which glands secrete by holocrine?Sebaceous glands
  6. Which glands secrete by merocrine?Eccrine sweat glands

4.1The four primary tissue types

  1. The four primary tissue typesEpithelial, connective, muscle, nervous
  2. Epithelial tissueCovers and lines body surfaces and forms glands; minimal extracellular matrix; rests on a basement membrane
  3. Connective tissueSupports and connects tissues; sparse cells suspended in abundant extracellular matrix
  4. Muscle tissueGenerates force and movement; skeletal, cardiac and smooth
  5. Nervous tissueSenses stimuli and transmits electrical signals; neurons and glia
  6. Which tissue has the most extracellular matrix?Connective tissue
  7. Which tissue has minimal extracellular matrix?Epithelial tissue

4.2Connective tissue classification

  1. Loose (areolar) connective tissueFew fibers and abundant ground substance; the packing tissue beneath epithelia
  2. Dense irregular connective tissueInterwoven collagen bundles; the dermis and organ capsules
  3. Dense regular connective tissueParallel, densely packed collagen bundles; tendons and ligaments
  4. Reticular connective tissueA mesh of type III reticular fibers scaffolding lymphoid organs and bone marrow
  5. Which connective tissue is in tendons and ligaments?Dense regular
  6. Which connective tissue is in the dermis?Dense irregular
