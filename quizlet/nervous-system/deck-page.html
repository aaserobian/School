1.1Anatomic components of the CNS and PNS

3 exam alerts
  1. Central nervous system (CNS)The brain, housed in the cranial cavity, and the spinal cord, housed in the vertebral canal
  2. Peripheral nervous system (PNS)12 pairs of cranial nerves, 31 pairs of spinal nerves, and ganglia
  3. GanglionA cluster of neuron cell bodies in the PNS
  4. The three functional subdivisions of the PNSSomatic, autonomic and enteric
  5. The three meninges, outermost to innermostDura mater, arachnoid mater, pia mater
  6. Mnemonic for the meningesalertDAP: Dura, Arachnoid, Pia, from the outside in
  7. What does the subarachnoid space contain?Cerebrospinal fluid (CSF)
  8. Cluster of neuron cell bodies: CNS vs. PNSalertNucleus in the CNS; ganglion in the PNS
  9. Bundle of axons: CNS vs. PNSalertTract in the CNS; nerve in the PNS
  10. Where is the brain housed?The cranial cavity
  11. Where is the spinal cord housed?The vertebral canal

1.2Functional areas of the brain and spinal cord

5 exam alerts
  1. ForebrainThe cerebrum (cortex, subcortical basal nuclei, white matter) and the diencephalon
  2. The two parts of the diencephalonThalamus, for sensory and motor relay, and hypothalamus, for homeostasis
  3. ThalamusSensory and motor relay
  4. HypothalamusHomeostasis
  5. CerebellumCoordinates posture, balance and motor planning
  6. The three layers of the cerebellar cortexGranular, Purkinje, molecular
  7. Purkinje cellsThe sole output of the cerebellar cortex; GABAergic, therefore inhibitory
  8. The three parts of the brainstemMidbrain, pons, medulla oblongata
  9. Frontal lobePrimary motor cortex (precentral gyrus), motor planning, executive function, working memory and Broca's area
  10. Parietal lobePrimary somatosensory cortex (postcentral gyrus), spatial awareness and integration of sensory inputs
  11. Temporal lobePrimary auditory cortex, Wernicke's area and memory (hippocampus)
  12. Occipital lobePrimary visual cortex (V1) and the visual association areas
  13. Precentral gyrusalertThe primary motor cortex, in the frontal lobe
  14. Postcentral gyrusalertThe primary somatosensory cortex, in the parietal lobe
  15. Broca's areaalertSpeech production; frontal lobe of the dominant hemisphere
  16. Wernicke's areaalertLanguage comprehension; temporal lobe of the dominant hemisphere
  17. Spinal cord gray matterMedial and butterfly or H-shaped
  18. Dorsal (posterior) hornSensory
  19. Ventral (anterior) hornMotor
  20. Lateral horn (T1 to L2)Sympathetic preganglionic cell bodies
  21. Spinal cord white matterLateral: ascending sensory tracts and descending motor tracts
  22. Gray vs. white matter organizationalertBrain: gray matter outer (cortex), white matter inner. Spinal cord: gray matter inner and medial, white matter outer and lateral.

1.3Nerves and ganglia in the PNS

4 exam alerts
  1. Connective tissue layers of a nerve, innermost to outermostEndoneurium, perineurium, epineurium
  2. EndoneuriumSurrounds an individual axon
  3. PerineuriumSurrounds a fascicle of axons
  4. EpineuriumSurrounds the entire nerve
  5. How many pairs of spinal nerves are there?31 pairs
  6. Breakdown of the 31 spinal nerve pairs8 cervical, 12 thoracic, 5 lumbar, 5 sacral, 1 coccygeal
  7. Are spinal nerves sensory, motor or mixed?All spinal nerves are mixed
  8. Which cranial nerves are CNS extensions?CN I and CN II. CN III to CN XII are PNS nerves.
  9. Sympathetic outflowalertThoracolumbar: T1 to L2
  10. Parasympathetic outflowalertCraniosacral: CN III, VII, IX, X and S2 to S4
  11. Sympathetic pathwayShort preganglionic axon to a sympathetic chain ganglion, then a long postganglionic axon releasing norepinephrine
  12. Exception to sympathetic norepinephrine releaseSweat glands, where the postganglionic fiber releases acetylcholine
  13. Parasympathetic pathwayLong preganglionic axon to a terminal ganglion near the target, then a short postganglionic axon releasing acetylcholine onto muscarinic receptors
  14. Which nerve carries about 75% of parasympathetic output?CN X, the vagus nerve
  15. SAME DAVEalertSensory = Afferent, Motor = Efferent; Dorsal = Afferent, Ventral = Efferent
  16. Where do sensory cell bodies sit?alertThe dorsal root ganglion (DRG)

1.4Categories of neurons

3 exam alerts
  1. Multipolar neuronOne axon plus multiple dendrites; the most common type, including motor and cortical neurons
  2. Bipolar neuronOne axon plus one dendrite; retinal, olfactory and vestibulocochlear neurons
  3. Pseudounipolar neuronA single process that bifurcates into central and peripheral axons; the sensory neurons of the DRG
  4. What morphology are DRG neurons?alertPseudounipolar, both the somatic and the visceral sensory neurons
  5. Glutamatergic neuronsRelease glutamate, the primary excitatory neurotransmitter of the CNS
  6. GABAergic neuronsRelease GABA; the primary inhibitory interneurons of the brain
  7. Glycinergic neuronsRelease glycine; the primary inhibitory interneurons of the spinal cord and brainstem
  8. Chief excitatory neurotransmitter of the CNSalertGlutamate
  9. Chief inhibitory neurotransmitters of the CNSalertGABA in the brain and glycine in the spinal cord

1.5Neuronal organelles, energetics and metabolism

1 exam alert
  1. Soma (perikaryon)The cell body: nucleus, Nissl bodies and organelles
  2. Nissl bodiesRough endoplasmic reticulum, where protein synthesis happens
  3. How are neuronal proteins degraded?By lysosomes and by proteasomes, which are ubiquitin-dependent
  4. What share of the body's oxygen and energy does the brain use?About 20%
  5. Astrocyte-neuron lactate shuttleAstrocytes convert glucose into lactate and shuttle it to neurons, which use it to make ATP by oxidative phosphorylation
  6. What energy substrate do astrocytes supply to neurons?alertLactate

1.6Specialized processes and axonal transport

3 exam alerts
  1. Axon initial segmentThe trigger zone, where action potentials originate
  2. What do microtubules do in the axon?Serve as the tracks for fast axonal transport
  3. Anterograde transportSoma to axon terminal, powered by kinesin
  4. Retrograde transportAxon terminal to soma, powered by dynein
  5. KinesinalertThe motor protein for anterograde transport, soma to terminal
  6. DyneinalertThe motor protein for retrograde transport, terminal to soma
  7. Electrical synapsesGap junctions made of connexons: bidirectional, ultrafast, with no synaptic delay
  8. Chemical synapsesNeurotransmitter released into a synaptic cleft: unidirectional, with a delay
  9. Electrical vs. chemical synapsesalertElectrical: bidirectional, no delay. Chemical: unidirectional, 0.5 to 1 ms delay.
  10. How wide is the synaptic cleft?20 to 40 nm
  11. How long is the chemical synaptic delay?0.5 to 1 ms
  12. Ionotropic vs. metabotropic receptorsIonotropic: fast ligand-gated ion channels. Metabotropic: G-protein-coupled.

1.7CNS and PNS glial cells

2 exam alerts
  1. Most abundant glial cell of the CNSAstrocytes
  2. Astrocyte functionsBlood-brain barrier perivascular end-feet, glutamate uptake, potassium regulation, the tripartite synapse, and scar formation by reactive gliosis
  3. How do astrocytes prevent excitotoxicity?They express EAAT1 and EAAT2 to take up glutamate
  4. Reactive gliosisScar formation by astrocytes
  5. OligodendrocytesMyelinate CNS axons; one cell myelinates segments of up to about 50 different axons
  6. Schwann cellsMyelinate PNS axons; each cell myelinates a single segment of ONE axon
  7. Myelination: CNS vs. PNSalertOligodendrocyte: one cell to about 50 axons. Schwann cell: one cell to one axon segment.
  8. MicrogliaThe resident immune and phagocytic cells, about 15 to 20% of brain cells
  9. Where do microglia come from?alertYolk-sac progenitors, so mesodermal, unlike the macroglia, which are ectodermal
  10. Ependymal cellsCiliated cells lining the ventricles
  11. What produces CSF?Specialized ependymal cells of the choroid plexus
  12. Satellite cellsEnsheath and support the cell bodies in PNS ganglia

2.1Stages, timeline and germ layers

1 exam alert
  1. Which germ layer forms the nervous system?alertEctoderm: the entire nervous system
  2. Developmental sequence of the nervous systemGastrulation, neural induction, neurulation, proliferation and migration, axonal outgrowth, synaptogenesis, refinement, myelination
  3. Which parts of development are hardwired and which are experiential?Early formation follows hardwired genetic programs; synapse refinement and pruning are experience-dependent

2.2Primary and secondary brain vesicles

2 exam alerts
  1. The 3 primary brain vesicles (weeks 3 to 4)Prosencephalon (forebrain), mesencephalon (midbrain), rhombencephalon (hindbrain)
  2. When do the 5 secondary vesicles appear?About week 5
  3. Which secondary vesicles come from the prosencephalon?Telencephalon and diencephalon
  4. Which secondary vesicles come from the rhombencephalon?Metencephalon and myelencephalon
  5. Telencephalon derivativesCerebral cortex, basal ganglia, hippocampus, olfactory bulbs, amygdala
  6. Telencephalon lumenThe lateral ventricles
  7. Diencephalon derivativesThalamus, hypothalamus and the optic vesicles, which give the optic nerve and retina
  8. Diencephalon lumenThe third ventricle
  9. Mesencephalon derivativesThe midbrain: tectum and tegmentum
  10. Mesencephalon lumenThe cerebral aqueduct
  11. Which primary vesicle does NOT divide?alertThe mesencephalon
  12. Metencephalon derivativesPons and cerebellum
  13. Metencephalon lumenThe fourth ventricle, upper part
  14. Myelencephalon derivativesThe medulla oblongata
  15. Myelencephalon lumenThe fourth ventricle, lower part
  16. Caudal neural tube derivativeThe spinal cord, whose lumen is the central canal
  17. Match each vesicle to its ventriclealertTelencephalon: lateral ventricles. Diencephalon: third ventricle. Mesencephalon: cerebral aqueduct. Metencephalon and myelencephalon: fourth ventricle.

2.3Cellular mechanisms of neural development

3 exam alerts
  1. Neural inductionThe notochord secretes morphogens instructing the overlying ectoderm to become the neural plate
  2. When does neurulation begin?About day 18
  3. When does the anterior neuropore close?About day 24
  4. When does the posterior neuropore close?About day 26
  5. Neural crest, the fourth germ layerA migratory progenitor population excluded at neural tube closure
  6. Neural crest derivativesMost of the PNS, including DRG sensory neurons, postganglionic autonomic neurons, Schwann cells and satellite cells, plus melanocytes and the adrenal medulla
  7. Neural tube vs. neural crestalertNeural tube forms the CNS. Neural crest forms the PNS, melanocytes and the adrenal medulla.
  8. Sonic hedgehog (SHH)Secreted by the notochord and floor plate; produces the ventral basal plate and its motor neurons
  9. BMPs in neural patterningSecreted by the roof plate; produce the dorsal alar plate and its sensory neurons
  10. Basal plateVentral, motor, induced by SHH
  11. Alar plateDorsal, sensory, induced by BMPs
  12. Alar vs. basal platealertAlar: sensory, dorsal, BMP. Basal: motor, ventral, SHH.
  13. How many developing neurons die by apoptosis?About one third to one half
  14. What do axons compete for?Target-derived neurotrophic factors such as NGF and BDNF
  15. Is synapse formation activity-dependent?No: synaptogenesis is activity-INDEPENDENT
  16. Is synapse refinement activity-dependent?Yes: refinement and pruning are activity-DEPENDENT
  17. The synapse rulealertFormation is activity-INDEPENDENT; refinement and pruning are activity-DEPENDENT

2.4Congenital birth defects and teratogens

2 exam alerts
  1. Failed anterior neuropore closureAnencephaly: absence of major brain structures, incompatible with sustained life
  2. AnencephalyFailure of the anterior neuropore to close, leaving major brain structures absent
  3. Failed posterior neuropore closureSpina bifida
  4. The spina bifida spectrumOcculta, then meningocele, then myelomeningocele
  5. Neuropore defectsalertAnterior failure gives anencephaly; posterior failure gives spina bifida
  6. What protects against neural tube defects?alertPericonceptional folic acid, or folate, supplementation
  7. Prenatal critical period for teratogensAbout 2 to 18 weeks, when exposure causes severe structural malformations
  8. Fetal alcohol spectrum disorder (FASD)The leading preventable cause of intellectual disability
